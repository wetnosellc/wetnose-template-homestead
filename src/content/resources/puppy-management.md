Bringing home a new puppy is exciting — and a little overwhelming. Here's what we recommend to set your puppy up for success.

## The First Night

Expect some crying the first few nights as your puppy adjusts to their new home. Place a worn t-shirt with your scent in their sleeping area. A crate next to your bed helps them feel less alone while keeping them safe.

## Crate Training

We strongly recommend crate training from day one. The crate is your puppy's safe den — never use it as punishment. Start with short periods while you're home, gradually increasing duration. Most puppies adapt quickly.

## House Training

Take your puppy outside:
- First thing in the morning
- After every meal
- After naps
- Before bed
- Every 1–2 hours in between

Praise and reward immediately when they eliminate outside. Accidents will happen — clean them up without scolding.

## Feeding Schedule

Feed your puppy **3 times per day** until 6 months, then transition to twice daily. We will provide the food your puppy has been eating — keep them on the same food initially and transition slowly if you choose to switch.

## Vet Care

Schedule a vet appointment within **48–72 hours** of bringing your puppy home. Your puppy will need:
- A general wellness exam
- Core vaccines (if due)
- Fecal test for parasites
- Discussion of spay/neuter timing

## Exercise

Young puppies need **controlled exercise**. Avoid excessive running, jumping, or stairs until growth plates close (typically 12–18 months). Short play sessions and gentle walks are ideal.

## Socialization Checklist

Expose your puppy to:
- [ ] Different people (men, women, children, hats, beards, uniforms)
- [ ] Other vaccinated dogs and puppies
- [ ] Different surfaces (grass, gravel, tile, stairs)
- [ ] Vehicles, loud noises, vacuums
- [ ] Handling all body parts (ears, paws, mouth)
